Team projects

25 students, five teams of five, five open problems — each built on a soft robot that already exists in the IITGN lab. Nothing is a toy problem, and nothing is starting from a blank sheet.

How it's structured

From open problem to demonstration

Teams are announced at the start of the week, and every taught session that follows is designed to feed directly into the project — the school's theory isn't a separate track from the build.

Tuesday

Design and BoM

After a survey of current solutions, each team turns its assigned open problem into a project design and a bill of materials — the first concrete commitment of the week.

Wednesday

Methods and geometry

Sensing and estimation lands the same day as methods planning — teams apply the geometry and kinematics they've just learned directly to their own project.

Thursday

Mechanics and control in SOFA

PCC kinematics and statics, worked through on paper, then applied as control in SOFA on the team's own robot.

Friday

Demonstration and review

Kinematic control, final project work, and a demonstration and review in front of the school closes the week.

Open, on purpose

Why there's no fixed list of problems here yet

Each team's open problem is drawn from real, unsolved questions on the robots already built in the IITGN soft robotics lab — not a pre-scripted challenge. The specific five problems, and the robots they're built around, will be announced closer to the school so they can be matched to the robots and mentors actually available in December.

What stays constant: design thinking, technical writing and communication are taught throughout the week, every day closes with a reflection session, and Friday ends with every team demonstrating and defending their work in front of the school.
